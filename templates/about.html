College Machine Learning Laboratory (MLM) Capstone

NewsGuard: Explainable Fake News, Clickbait & Headline–Body Mismatch Detection

A multi-faceted forensic Natural Language Processing architecture designed to combat online information disorders through verifiable machine learning models and transparent algorithmic explainability.

1. Problem Statement

Digital journalism and social feeds have been inundated by three distinct but overlapping information pathologies:

  • Fabricated Disinformation (Fake News): Deliberately false articles mimicking real journalism to deceive readers or manipulate democratic processes.
  • Clickbait Deception: Sensational, hyperbolic, or suspense-driven headlines crafted to monetize clicks, degrading public trust in media.
  • Headline–Body Mismatch (Bait-and-Switch): Sensational headlines paired with ordinary or unrelated articles, misleading readers who only skim titles.

Existing systems treat "fake news" as a monolithic black-box label without explaining why an article was flagged or distinguishing sensational framing from factual fraud.

2. Project Objectives
  • Disentangled Multi-Task Analysis: Decouple fake news classification from clickbait detection and semantic alignment rather than conflating them into a single score.
  • Transparent Explainability: Expose local feature attributions, influential vocabulary weights, and specific rhetorical tropes behind every prediction.
  • Calibrated Lexical Consistency: Establish an empirically validated decision threshold for headline–body similarity using TF-IDF cosine similarity.
  • Verifiable External Grounding: Integrate real-time encyclopedic reference retrieval to contextualize named entities and core claims.
  • Academic Integrity: Train models strictly on published benchmark datasets with held-out test evaluations and transparent scientific boundaries.
3. Proposed Methodology & Forensic Pipeline Architecture

NewsGuard processes an article through a multi-stage pipeline where text is cleaned, tokenized, vectorized, and passed through three independent machine learning modules alongside an external retrieval check.

5. Benchmark Datasets & Origins
Fake or Real News Dataset George McIntire & Lutz Hamel (Univ. of Rhode Island). 6,295 verified articles with balanced real journalism (Reuters, NYT) vs disinformation.
Stop Clickbait Dataset Chakraborty et al. (ASONAM 2016). 32,000 headlines (16k clickbait from BuzzFeed/Upworthy vs 16k non-clickbait from NYT/Guardian/Hindu).
Headline–Body Mismatch Benchmark Formulated using paired articles vs synthetically swapped bodies to evaluate bait-and-switch deception at calibrated threshold τ=0.100.
6. System Boundaries & Limitations
  • Lexical vs Physical Reality: Statistical text classification detects stylistic and vocabulary cues. It cannot physically verify newly unfolding events without external corroboration.
  • Sensationalism ≠ Falsity: Sensational headlines are frequent in entertainment journalism and sports without being factually false.
  • Temporal Drift: Models trained on specific news cycles (e.g. 2016) reflect contemporary political discourse and require periodic retraining.
7. Future Enhancements
  • Transformer Fine-Tuning: Fine-tune RoBERTa or DeBERTa models for cross-encoder Natural Language Inference (NLI) stance detection.
  • Multi-Modal Forensics: Evaluate reverse image search and image-text consistency to detect deepfakes and repurposed imagery.
  • Knowledge Graph Grounding: Query structured knowledge graphs (Wikidata, DBpedia) for automated triple verification.
